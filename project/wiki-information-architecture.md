# MyNDIS wiki information architecture

This document captures the working public-site structure for MyNDIS. It is intentionally organised around what a person needs to do, not around the organisational structure of the NDIA.

## Homepage

The homepage should present two primary entry points prominently and with minimal competing information:

1. **I want to apply to the NDIS.**
2. **I am an NDIS participant.**

Secondary discovery methods, including Questions and Keywords, can remain available without competing with those two primary choices.

## Design principles

- Keep as much of each practical process on one page as is reasonably usable. Avoid reproducing the NDIS website pattern of sending users through many fragmented pages.
- Use breakout keyword/concept pages for definitions and deeper cross-site exploration without interrupting the main task flow.
- Link clearly to relevant official NDIS pages and preserved primary-source material.
- Prefer checklists, timelines, downloadable templates, and concrete next actions where they are genuinely useful.
- Design mobile-first.
- Maintain a clear distinction between MyNDIS guidance and official government material.

## Apply

### Eligibility

- **Am I eligible?** step-by-step quiz.
- Explain access requirements on one main page wherever possible.
- Use keyword pages for terms that require definition or wider context.
- If a person appears not to be eligible, point them to other national and state/territory resources that may help.
- If a person appears eligible, introduce organisations that may be able to help with an application.

### Application process

Create a detailed one-page application guide supported by breakout keyword pages and links to official NDIS sources.

Potential tools and resources:

- Official NDIS application documents available for download.
- Where lawful and appropriate, convenience bundles that combine multiple official PDFs without changing their substantive content.
- Letter templates for doctors and allied health professionals.
- A downloadable folder/package containing the materials a person may need to prepare an application.
- Application checklist.
- Evidence catalogue template for medical records and other supporting evidence.
- Post-submission process explanation and checklist.

### After submission

Explain clearly:

- how a person finds out whether they have been approved;
- expected stages after submission;
- when it may be appropriate to check in;
- how to respond to requests for additional information;
- what happens after approval and the likely sequence of next steps;
- what to do after rejection;
- review/appeal pathways after rejection; and
- other services and resources that may help someone who is not accepted onto the NDIS.

## Participant

### Understanding a plan

- Understanding the NDIS decision letter and plan.
- Plan period and dates.
- Self-managed, plan-managed, and agency-managed funding, with detailed practical differences.
- Funding categories at a much more granular level than the standard NDIS overview.
- Support coordination examples and options.
- Planning meeting expectations, preparation checklist, and questions to consider in advance.
- What to do if unhappy with a plan, including review/appeal pathways.

### Identifying and finding supports

- How to identify supports a participant might need or want.
- Questions a participant can work through alone, with a doctor, or with allied health professionals.
- How to locate supports.
- How to evaluate potential providers and independent workers.
- Working with a company versus an independent worker.
- Customising care around the participant's actual needs.
- More accessible summaries of costs and price limits than relying only on large NDIS PDFs.
- Rough examples of how hours, funding, and billing interact.

### Funding and claims

- Explain funding periods.
- Explain flexible funding categories.
- Explain the money flow for self-management, plan management, and agency management.
- How to make a claim.
- How to use the NDIS portal.
- What to put in the service-description field when making a claim.
- NDIS iOS and Android apps.
- How invoicing works and how to understand invoice elements.
- Service agreements with support workers, including boilerplate/example contractual language where appropriate.
- What happens if a claim is rejected.
- What happens if funding runs out.
- Typical refund/payment timing where reliable information is available.
- What the NDIS cannot fund, including the boundary with medical/health-system responsibilities.

### Assessments, modifications, and specific supports

- Functional Capacity Assessments.
- Home modifications: assessments, process, and application requirements.
- Vehicle/car modifications: assessments, process, and application requirements.
- Transport budget payments.
- Assistive technology.
- Consumables.

### People and roles

Explain the roles and distinctions between:

- NDIA and NDIS;
- planner;
- Local Area Coordinator / Local Access Coordinator terminology where applicable;
- support coordinator;
- internal review officer;
- nominee; and
- other NDIA roles a participant may encounter.

Also cover:

- contacting the NDIA;
- record-keeping best practices;
- participant "check-ins"; and
- nominee arrangements for assistance.

### Higher-support needs

Research and develop practical information for people who need substantial support, including:

- 24-hour care;
- supported living / group-home options;
- temporary accommodation/stays;
- respite opportunities; and
- other high-intensity support arrangements.

This area requires dedicated research before publication.

## Problems and changes

Create a clearly visible Problems section covering:

- change of circumstances;
- plan reassessment/review;
- plan ending and rollover/extension;
- appealing a decision;
- tribunal proceedings and external review (requires research);
- technical review teams or equivalent internal processes where applicable;
- FOI process;
- complaints about the NDIA;
- complaints about providers/supports;
- fraud reporting;
- when and how the NDIA contacts participants;
- gathering ongoing evidence;
- checklist for reports written by support providers/allied health professionals; and
- changes coming to the NDIS/NDIA.

## Keeping up to date

Potential resources:

- official email/newsletter sign-ups;
- legislation tracking;
- media tracking;
- NDIA/NDIS press-release index;
- general NDIS news index;
- Reddit and Facebook communities, clearly distinguished from authoritative sources;
- advocacy organisations;
- free legal resources; and
- other relevant federal services such as DSP, Health Care Cards/concession cards, and Centrelink programs.

## Children and NDIS

Create dedicated information for children and families, including a **Thriving Kids** page that records all reliable available information nationally and, where relevant, state-by-state.

This section should distinguish confirmed policy/program details from proposals, announcements, and unresolved implementation questions.

## Urgent help outside the NDIS

Create a practical page explaining where to seek urgent help when the NDIS is not the appropriate service, including:

- urgent mental-health support pathways such as local crisis/CATT services where applicable;
- physical-health virtual emergency departments where available;
- urgent care clinics;
- after-hours GPs; and
- telehealth options.

Because service availability differs by location and changes over time, this content will require careful sourcing and jurisdiction-specific maintenance.

## Official documents and convenience downloads

MyNDIS should make official forms and documents easier to locate. Where copyright and government-use terms allow, the site may also provide convenience packages such as grouped downloads or combined PDFs, provided that:

- the source documents are clearly identified as official documents;
- substantive wording is not altered;
- provenance and source links are preserved;
- the date/version is clear; and
- MyNDIS does not present a modified convenience copy as an original government-issued file.

Legal/licensing details should be checked before redistributing modified or combined official files.
